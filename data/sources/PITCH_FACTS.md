## Ready-to-use problem statement (2026 framing)

> The Judiciary of Tanzania has cut its backlog to about 5% of pending cases, and in 2025 a Primary Court case took 30 days on average from filing to decision. What remains is concentrated: 84.5% of the backlog is at the Court of Appeal, where a case takes 816 days on average (Judiciary of Tanzania, 2025). Much of the delay comes from outside the court: in the prosecution files the National Audit Office sampled, 60% of adjournments were for incomplete investigation and the rest for the State Attorney appearing without the case status or file (NAO, 2022), and 28.7% of people in prison are unconvicted remand detainees (World Prison Brief, 2024). The task now is to keep courts on target: to see the few cases sliding towards an overrun early, and to put each external delay in front of the agency that can clear it.

### Earlier framing (backlog-reduction)

> Tanzania's courts carry a long tail of delay that no one can see coming. The National Audit Office found that criminal cases in the High Court took an average of four years before their first trial session, with 13 Dar es Salaam cases running past 49 months (NAO, 2022). As of June 2024 the Court of Appeal alone had 5,900 pending cases, 1,248 of them unresolved for more than two years (Judiciary of Tanzania, via Afrobarometer, 2026). Fewer than half of Tanzanians (47%) expect court cases to be resolved within a reasonable time (Afrobarometer, 2026), and 28.7% of people in prison are unconvicted remand detainees (World Prison Brief, 2024). Registrars still build cause lists by hand, with no early warning of which cases are sliding towards backlog.
