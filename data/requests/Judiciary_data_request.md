## Request for anonymised, aggregated case-flow statistics for JusticeFlow TZ

We are building JusticeFlow TZ for the eGA-RIDC AI & Data Science Hackathon. It is a decision-support tool that estimates a case's risk of becoming backlog and helps registrars prepare cause lists. It is read-only: it would never write to JoT-eCMS or JSDS2, and nothing it proposes takes effect without a registrar's approval.

Our prototype currently runs on synthetic data fitted to published figures: the Judiciary's disposition targets and 2020 backlog shares, and the 2025 filing statistics announced by the Chief Registrar. To check it against real case flow, we would like to request **aggregated, anonymised statistics only**. We are not asking for case files, names, or any personal data.

### What we are asking for

For a recent period (for example 2023–2025), broken down by **court level** and **case type** (civil / criminal / land / matrimonial / probate / labour / commercial) where available:

1. **Time to disposal:** number of cases decided, grouped into bands of 0–6, 6–12, 12–24 and over 24 months from filing.
2. **Hearings and adjournments:** average number of hearings and of adjournments per decided case.
3. **Adjournment reasons:** counts by recorded reason (e.g. advocate request, prosecution not ready, investigation incomplete, awaiting forensic or expert report, magistrate unavailable).
4. **Pending cases by age:** pending cases per level grouped into the same age bands, as at a given date.
5. **Legal representation:** share of cases where a party had an advocate, if recorded.

Figures at national or judicial-zone level are enough. We do not need region or court-station detail. Where a group would contain fewer than 10 cases, please suppress or merge it.

### How the data would be used and protected
- Used only to calibrate and test the JusticeFlow TZ prototype, and reported only in aggregate.
- Not shared with third parties, and deleted at the end of the hackathon unless the Judiciary agrees otherwise in writing.
- Any published result would credit the Judiciary of Tanzania as the source, and we would share our findings with the Judiciary first.

We would be glad to present the prototype to the Data Hub team. If a formal data-sharing agreement is needed, we are happy to follow the Judiciary's procedure.
